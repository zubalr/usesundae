# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Sundae is for founders, product designers, and product managers reviewing a rendered web product before deciding what to improve. They use it with their own ChatGPT in a WebMCP-capable browser, or through the same visible human controls when Site Tools are unavailable.

## Product Purpose

Sundae helps a person and their ChatGPT audit the same product together. It identifies the product category and job from the rendered experience and the person's optional goal, then evaluates whether the interface communicates and delivers that job through its visual system, UX architecture, interactions, responsiveness, and observed motion. Success is a small set of genuine, evidence-linked recommendations that would materially improve the product if implemented, not a required quota of faults.

## Positioning

Sundae turns the audit itself into a shared WebMCP workspace. ChatGPT operates page-hosted Site Tools against the same visible board the person sees; measured browser facts, product judgments, unseen coverage, human decisions, reversible previews, and fresh verification remain distinct. The page is the inspectable control layer rather than a hidden report generated elsewhere.

## Operating Context

The guaranteed contest path is the included `/demo` workspace. Public HTTPS products can be captured through the configured browser provider after visible approval. Evidence-derived same-origin pages can add category and journey context without becoming an unbounded crawl. ChatGPT Desktop's built-in browser discovers Site Tools from the open page without a plugin; ordinary browsers retain the human workflow.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The audit begins with the product's job, audience, category, and visible value delivery before looking for faults.
- Visual hierarchy, composition, typography, spacing, color, responsive behavior, information architecture, interaction feedback, flow clarity, and observed motion are first-class audit material.
- Screenshots, geometry, rendered copy, accessibility summaries, and DOM facts support judgments; they do not replace product reasoning.
- Markup hierarchy and other code-level details stay low priority or are omitted unless they create a demonstrated user, navigation, or assistive-technology problem.
- Severity reflects likely user and product impact in the observed context, not rule type. Sundae does not invent a problem when the evidence shows a strong implementation.
- Important unseen routes, states, breakpoints, interactions, or motion windows remain explicit coverage gaps.
- A preview is local and reversible. A finding is verified only after a fresh recapture of the same relevant scope.
- Public capture is limited to pages the configured provider can render. Sundae does not silently crawl, use target-site cookies, log in, submit forms, or claim coverage it did not observe.
- Sundae does not infer conversion, revenue, retention, security, legal compliance, SEO rank, or backend correctness from rendered UI evidence alone.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is Sundae. The public story is WebMCP-first, direct, design-literate, and honest about uncertainty. It must demonstrate the product rather than decorate the page with generic AI imagery, inflated scores, invented customers, or repeated evidence-report furniture.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains a reproducible same-origin demo with measurable baseline and improved states, a visible shared workbench, page-hosted WebMCP tools, and bounded public-browser capture. There are no customer testimonials, commercial benchmarks, or exhaustive-coverage claims to publish.

## Product Principles

1. Understand the product before criticizing the interface.
2. Judge the rendered experience, using technical facts as receipts rather than the whole audit.
3. Prefer a few consequential truths to a long generic defect list.
4. Keep human authority and agent actions visible on the same surface.
5. Say “not seen” until fresh evidence justifies a stronger claim.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Accessibility evidence matters when it changes whether someone can perceive, understand, navigate, or operate the product. Findings should explain that concrete effect and use proportionate severity; semantic or markup differences without demonstrated experience impact are not high-severity design findings.
